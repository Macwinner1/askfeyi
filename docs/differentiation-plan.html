Ground rules

What actually creates exposure

I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice. But the engineering side of it is simple enough to state plainly.

Copyright protects expression: the specific sentences, images, and code someone wrote. It does not protect ideas or functionality. Nobody owns the concept of paying bills over WhatsApp, an FAQ accordion, or a three-column section. Common layout conventions are weak ground for a claim precisely because everyone uses them.

So a section called "Frequently asked questions" is fine. A section whose answers are their answers is not. Swapping the trade name inside an otherwise identical paragraph is the clearest form of the problem, not a defence against it.

The safest position is also the better product position: write from your own facts. Your limits, your fees, your partner, your users. Copy that is specific to Feyi cannot be anybody else's by definition.

The wedge

Different audience, not different decoration

Feyi is for people who run something. Zahia is for everybody.

Look at what the product already does: bulk transfers to several vendors at once, bulk airtime across a staff list, scheduled rent, a spending summary at the end of the day. Those are not consumer features. They are the daily admin of somebody running a shop, a small team, or a trade.

Zahia's site aims at everyone with money, which is why it reads as generic. Pointing Feyi at operators is a genuine positioning difference, it is supported by features you have already built, and it makes copied copy impossible, because none of their sentences are written for that reader.

Architecture

A page shaped by that decision

Not the SaaS template with different words. The running order below follows the order an operator actually evaluates a money app.

  1. One conversation, doing a day's work

    A live chat in the hero paying three vendors in a single message. Not a feature list, a task completing.

  2. The receipt

    Nigerian trade runs on proof of payment. Every Feyi transaction produces a receipt you forward to the person you paid. Zahia has receipt images in their assets and never builds anything around them. This is the most ownable idea available and nobody is using it.

  3. Pay everyone at once

    The bulk capability, given its own section instead of one card in a grid of six.

  4. Who holds your money

    Partner, licence, and the plain sentence that Feyi never holds it.

  5. What it costs

    Answered directly. Every competitor dodges this, which is exactly why answering reads as confidence.

  6. When it goes wrong

    Lost phone, wrong recipient, a misread instruction, how a dispute resolves. Where sceptics convert.

  7. Questions, written from your support inbox

    Real questions people have actually asked you. This is what replaces the copied FAQ.

Identity

Ledger, not glass

Zahia's look is dark navy, glassmorphism, blurred emerald glows, Inter. Our current page still shares the last of that: the frosted card treatment is the one piece of their visual language left in the stylesheet.

The direction I would take instead comes from the receipt idea. Paper rather than glass. An off-white ground, ink black text, hairline rules, monospaced figures for every amount, and green reserved for the one action per screen. It looks like a ledger and a printed receipt, which is what the product produces, and it is impossible to confuse with a dark glassmorphic site.

  • paper
  • ink
  • action
  • secondary
  • rule

Type should move off Inter, which both sites use and which reads as the default choice. A grotesque with some character for headings, and tabular monospaced numerals wherever an amount appears, so figures line up the way they do on a real receipt.

Pushback

Where I would argue with this

Narrowing to operators loses consumer signups
It does, and that is the trade. A page addressed to everyone converts nobody in particular. If consumer volume is the actual business, say so and I will build the wedge around a different reader, but pick one.
The receipt may already exist in the product
If the Java backend already generates receipts, this is a marketing decision and cheap. If it does not, I am proposing a page that promises something the product does not do yet, which we must not ship. Confirm before phase three.
Rewriting the FAQ needs your support inbox
I can write replacements that are original and accurate, but the genuinely good version uses questions real users asked. If you have any WhatsApp support history, that is the raw material.
The limits need checking, not rewording
Publishing Zahia's transaction limits as your own is a product accuracy problem before it is a copyright one. Those figures need confirming against your own licence and partner agreement regardless of what we do to the page.
Plan only, nothing built. Phase one closes the live exposure and needs nothing from anybody, so I would start there unless you say otherwise.
